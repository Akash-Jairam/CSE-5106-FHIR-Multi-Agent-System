Generic V5 components
The reusable mechanism is immutable question/context + actual executed program + source handles; a bounded executable inspection; one explicit code replacement; full execution and original-source validation; preservation of a valid primary on nonfatal inability; strict accounting and a complete audit trail. Unique exact quotes with host-derived spans provide provenance without model character counting. Strict finite-JSON equality avoids conflating false/zero/null. No question-ID, family, answer-kind, threshold/ordinal repair grammar or model-ID routing appears in the V5 critic module. Missing information is not automatically an error, and there is no prefer-nonempty branch.

These are algorithmic safeguards, not semantic proofs. A probe can output a literal; a real source fact may be irrelevant to the claimed correction; one text region can change many downstream behaviors. Hiding rendered answers does not make V5 an independent blind verifier: its code and records can reveal them. The no-effect filter detects changed answer/evidence/contributors, not demonstrated semantic improvement. Those limits matter in every domain.

FHIR and benchmark adapter boundaries
The current implementation imports the FHIR resource-ID grammar/supported types, concrete indexed field paths, single-patient/context parser, retrieval ledger, closure/scope checks and check_fields. They must be understood as the TaskAdapter contract. Positive immutable source facts are mandatory; a context-only error, source-free arithmetic task or legitimate absence without a positive field witness is outside this critic's current repair coverage. Frozen retrieval prevents ungrounded extra records but also prevents recovery of a missing resource type. Count/rank/absence completeness is task dependent, not established by citation validity alone.

The unchanged shared guide also contains released-data conventions: MIMIC local coding, encounter-hosp/encounter-icu identifier suffixes and links, wall-clock comparison ignoring offsets, midnight relative windows, SQLite month overflow and selected medication time/name representations. The source checker recognizes a bounded English calendar/encounter/paired-scope grammar. These are not per-question gold routes, and sharing them with Single preserves the current comparison, but calling the entire substrate benchmark agnostic would be inaccurate. New wording, other languages, population/multi-patient tasks, another FHIR profile or another hospital can exceed this adapter.

One necessary primary-source cross-check confirms the boundary: FHIR R4 dateTime carries a timezone when hours/minutes are specified, and Observation.effective[x] supports dateTime, Period, Timing and instant. Therefore the current offset-ignoring and effectiveDateTime-focused conventions are a declared released-data interpretation, not a universal FHIR rule.

Model/runtime boundaries and transfer risks
The critic's JSON/quote/probe/patch protocol does not require model-specific weights, logits, finetuning or special semantic routes. But the substrate has provider/model compatibility branches (including Luna request options and Qwen tool parsing), uses cl100k_base for estimates, and assumes the current chat/tool/usage shape. Those are transport dependencies, not evidence of semantic generalization. Other tokenizers can change admission/coverage despite unchanged text; verified post-call usage and fatal overshoot handling protect accounting but do not make the current preflight estimates universally accurate.

The 175k/25k split, 27/3 tools, two critic calls, 5,500-token intact packet, 1,800/4,000 completions, one probe, positive-fact limits and 8k-region/32k-program bounds are explicit engineering choices. They may under-allocate a weaker model, exclude longer programs or suppress useful multi-step verification. Coverage measured on prior exposed packets is not unseen-question or cross-model accuracy evidence. Strict schema failure safely preserves the primary when accounting is known, but high format failure can eliminate all benefit. Unknown billed usage remains fatal and can lose a valid primary; this policy must be carried honestly into transfer results.

The two conditional alternatives
1. Two independent complete solves plus executable selection has a generic separation-of-histories principle. It does not depend intrinsically on FHIR, but its source and execution eligibility still need an adapter. Independent histories do not imply independent errors, especially with the same deterministic model. The proposed 90k/13 + 90k/13 + 20k/4 allocation can weaken both solvers; it is not established to transfer better than one capable 200k/30 solver. Two disagreeing answers have no majority. MBR-EXEC's many-candidate execution agreement supports a mechanism, not this two-sample clinical result:
https://aclanthology.org/2022.emnlp-main.231/

2. The 175k/25 + 25k/5 cross-form differential alternative has a generic idea: make programs disagree on explicit inputs and use a separately generated program as a fallible proxy. Its SQL/relational lowering is a structured-data adapter, not a universal QA representation; faithful FHIR flattening, source lineage, null/time/units/order semantics and valid fixture constraints are substantial requirements. It cannot be advertised as portable merely because both Python and SQL execute. A generated proxy can share the same mistaken interpretation, and distinguishing two programs does not identify the intended one. DPC's author implementation and tested SQL-to-Python direction do not establish the reverse Python-primary/SQL-challenger proposal. Its normalization metric must not be imported into typed/time-sensitive QA:

Post-run claim discipline
Keep ongoing V5 frozen. A completed win supports this tested cohort/model, subject to the planned once-only question-confirmation analysis; fixed300 adaptation and extensive patient overlap prevent a new-patient claim. That question stratum is also not an unseen-template, external-task or cross-model test. Portable core, faithful task adapter, compatible runtime and empirical transfer are four different assertions. Future model/task transfer should freeze the method and adapter assumptions before inspecting its test outputs, permit only declared compatibility changes, retain failures/unknowns and use a strong budget-matched control within each model. Equal token ceilings across models do not mean equal FLOPs, price or semantic work. No cross-model experiment is required now, and no evidence currently justifies “generalizes across models/tasks.”
