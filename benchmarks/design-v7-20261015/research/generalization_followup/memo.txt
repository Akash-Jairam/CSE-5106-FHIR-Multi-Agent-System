Bounded transfer assessment for V7, 2026-10-15
Research only; frozen running V7 unchanged. Four primary studies, not a new design or experiment. No actual project QA, results, references, protected questions, configuration, FHIR or model requests were accessed. Papers were rechecked online; prior author-code audits are explicitly distinguished from the code text reread here.

The defensible objective is a reusable orchestration contract with an empirically tested advantage in the present setting. Configuration independence and generic prompts are useful engineering properties, but neither establishes performance transfer. Separate three questions: can a fresh trajectory supply a complementary correct outcome; can selection recover it without harming correct A; and do those effects persist after changing the task/model? V7 tests the first two jointly under residual resources, on one model and one FHIR environment.

Four sources and their limits

1. AgentScaling, Kim et al., Nature Machine Intelligence (2026), with arXiv v3 inspected: https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-026-01268-y and https://arxiv.org/html/2512.08296v3 . The controlled study spans260 configurations, six agentic benchmarks, five architectures and three model families. Coordination benefit varies with task structure and strong-Single capability. Held-out configuration/within-domain architecture selection transfers better than absolute success prediction on an omitted domain. The paper's own cross-domain discussion limits the claim; its empirical capability threshold is not a universal routing rule. This is broad evidence against assuming one architecture wins everywhere, not a prediction that residual V7 must fail. Reused author-code pin: https://github.com/ybkim95/agent-scaling/tree/8673d6a8d5a764a07b08e4283492380d80257223 . The prior audit distinguishes article260 from270 including10 out-of-sample configurations. No numerical reproduction or exact environment parity is claimed.

2. Correlated Errors in Large Language Models, Kim et al., ICML2025, https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07962v1 . It studies over350 models across two multiple-choice leaderboards and resume screening, and finds substantial shared errors, including across providers. Its approximately60% HELM result is identical wrong-answer agreement CONDITIONAL ON BOTH WRONG, not a Pearson correlation or a universal probability for arbitrary agent trajectories. Its judge analysis shows shared error can inflate apparent correctness. These are measured multi-model outputs, not a controlled test of V7's fresh retrieval. Blindness and another model name therefore cannot be assumed to create independent errors. Reused author-code pin: https://github.com/nikhgarg/llm_correlated_errors_public/tree/30a2c0aa88af3f428f1f7034538373d24c209176 . Existing code audit is reused; no new analysis was run.

3. Benchmark Test-Time Scaling of General LLM Agents (ATTS/General AgentBench), Li et al., arXiv v1, https://arxiv.org/html/2602.18998v1 . The benchmark evaluates ten models; scaling figures cover five models across four domains. Independent trajectories improve oracle pass@K, but actual self-choice can plateau or regress; external GPT-5 pointwise judgment does not reliably remove the gap. Moving from specialized contexts to a unified tool environment affects models differently. This is direct evidence that candidate coverage and selection, and task/scaffold transfer, are separate. It does not test a single residual200k/30/30 bundle. Reused author-code pin: https://github.com/cxcscmu/General-AgentBench/tree/35f5c027c31ddcb3366b28674c6cb2957460c0e2 . Prior runner audit found K-1 tool-free pairwise calls and an optional compressed/minimal trajectory mode; its pointwise conditional metric must not be called selected-answer accuracy.

4. FineVerify, Zhao et al., arXiv v1, https://arxiv.org/html/2606.00660v1 . Positive evidence: across four search benchmarks, four-candidate average accuracy rises59.2→67.4 for GPT-5-mini and71.3→76.9 for Gemini-3-flash. Shared question-derived requirements plus separate evidence-search verification outperform lenient missing-evidence scoring. This is two-model replication within search, not task-independent transfer. Selection accuracy is conditioned on at least one correct candidate; V7's tool-free selector is weaker than a verifier acquiring evidence. Author-code pin: https://github.com/XuZhao0/fineverify/tree/4d172e30a51946437b7cb4c47617872c3fd346ee . Reread retained pinned tools.py and gpt5_mini_fineverify.py: the separate verifier prompt lacks references, but a reference-aware extraction/grading stage precedes verification and influences inputs/skips. That evaluation boundary, retries, native search costs and large output allowances cannot be copied into deployable V7 or treated as our accounting parity.

Constraints for interpreting the frozen V7 result

- Keep its unchanged full natural Single A and actual A-only comparison. Team>actualA measures net contribution of the B+selector stage on that recorded policy prefix; Team>freshSingle is a separate stochastic comparison. Neither alone establishes model/task transfer. Do not infer the selector caused a gain: V7 changes the candidate supply and selection together.
- Keep all300 first attempts, residual coverage skips, ineligible/prose A, malformed/failed B, unknown usage and fatal auxiliary loss. Conditional B-eligible accuracy or selector accuracy can look good while the whole system loses. Report complementary A-wrong/B-right supply separately from helpful/harmful switches and all post-A net effect. Agreement is not independent corroboration.
- Residual admission selects cases according to A's actual resource consumption. Another model or task can change both available B budget and packet/schema/source eligibility without changing the controller. Future transfer claims would need the same frozen policy and complete-denominator comparison on the named model/task, not merely a successful SDK/schema mock or a new model identifier. Token ceilings are an operational control, not equal hardware compute across model families.
- Preserve the generic orchestration/FHIR adapter boundary: unchanged common semantics and source checker apply to both agents/control; actual retrieval/execution/source provenance stays visible. Domain-specific check success establishes its stated support boundary, not answer correctness or retrieval completeness. Avoid model-name, template, answer-kind or exposed-case repair routing as a claimed general method.
- The exposed300 remains adaptive development evidence. The protected1397 can supply the existing once-only question-disjoint confirmation on Luna; it does not create new-patient, new-task or cross-model evidence. Full2931 performance remains evidence for this released benchmark. No new evaluation or access is proposed here, and no running V7 prompt, gate or budget should change in response to this memo.

Recommendation: interpret any V7 win as a bounded, model-specific result with a reusable implementation and measured mechanisms. Keep portability as a hypothesis. If future refinement is authorized, favor changes to evidence acquisition/feedback and accountable existing-candidate selection that have task-derived interfaces; require fresh prospective transfer evidence before claiming broader performance. The four papers justify this measurement discipline, not an expectation of winning or an extra experiment now.
