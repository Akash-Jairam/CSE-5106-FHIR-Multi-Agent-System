Judgment

The literature supports conditional gains, not a general agent-count advantage.
Useful additional proposals, accurate selection, retained primary capacity and
coordination cost are separate requirements. Architectural isolation is portable;
its accuracy advantage remains task/model dependent. Nothing below changes V6's
frozen protocol or prospective acceptance/confirmation rules.

1. Task structure and capability under controlled budgets

Kim et al., Capable language models can outgrow the benefits of collaboration,
Nature Machine Intelligence 8, 1157–1172; version of record 24 July 2026. The earlier
arXiv v3 (8 Apr 2026) retains Towards a Science of Scaling Agent Systems as its title.
The final article studies 260 configurations/six domains/five architectures/three
model families. Fairness uses matched per-system reasoning-token ceilings and
identical tool access; iteration limits are safety/termination ceilings, not the
primary fairness control. Single-agent baseline performance is the strongest
robust predictor. The empirical ~45% capability rule is not a universal scaling
law or permission for model/task-score routing. Trace-level error amplification
is distinct from statistical model-error correlation. Architecture ranking is
reported for held-out WITHIN-DOMAIN configurations; absolute unseen-domain
prediction is poor (leave-one-dataset-out R²=-2.09). Small coding/terminal samples
and only six domains further limit transfer. These controls do not establish
all-input/output/provider-cost matching to our 200k/30 experiment.

2. Negative evidence and tuning sensitivity

Smit et al., Should we be going MAD? ICML 2024; submitted 29 Nov 2023, v3 = 18 Jul 2024.
Debate does not reliably outperform self-consistency or non-debate ensembles,
and generally costs more calls/tokens. Forced disagreement can overturn a correct
answer. Counterevidence is real: tuning agreement prompts can make some debate
configurations outperform comparators. Most results use GPT-3.5; GPT-4/Mixtral
and domain checks are narrower. This is cost/time-aware evidence, NOT a strict
matched-compute experiment. It supports judging actual retained benefit rather
than assuming debate cannot work, or assuming a successful tuned configuration
will transfer. Protocol tuning itself needs protected confirmation.
https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/smit24a.html
https://arxiv.org/html/2311.17371v3

3. The evaluator must help the generator, not merely score well on preferences

Zhou et al., JETTS, ICML 2025; first 21 Apr 2025, inspected v2 = 21 May 2025. It evaluates
10 judges and 8 generators across math, coding and instruction following through
reranking, beam search and critique/refinement. Reranking can help, but utility
depends strongly on task and judge/generator strength; coding is particularly
difficult. Natural-language critique is not reliably useful for improvement.
The study measures generator benefit, not just agreement with a judge benchmark.
It includes 10-candidate pools and expensive pairwise comparisons; it does not
prove that V6's one-pass same-model A/B selector is reliable or compute optimal.
https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/zhou25af.html
https://arxiv.org/html/2504.15253v2

Official code pin 18c3e9e26deed2bd2d644164b2e1823389d8d1e7 (2 Jun 2026):
https://github.com/SalesforceAIResearch/jetts-benchmark/tree/18c3e9e26deed2bd2d644164b2e1823389d8d1e7
Inspected rerank/refinement and prompted_judge code. Default non-critique pairwise
judgment evaluates both AB and BA and aggregates results; that costs extra calls.
V6 has one fixed-position selector and cannot inherit that control for free.
Refinement separately generates critique, requests a revision, then reranks the
retained pool; it is not the same as our two-call recomputation/selection path.

4. Diversity labels are not independent errors

Kim, Garg, Peng and Garg, Correlated Errors in Large Language Models, ICML 2025;
first 9 Jun 2025. Across leaderboard models and a resume task, different providers/
architectures still exhibit shared errors; more accurate models can share errors
too. On HELM, the reported ~60% is identical wrong-answer agreement CONDITIONAL
ON BOTH BEING WRONG, not a 60% error rate or universal correlation coefficient.
The judge case substitutes a model's own answers for truth, demonstrating one
mechanism for biased evaluation; it is not a direct program-selection experiment.
Multiple-choice/task/population limits prevent estimating V6's correlation from
that number. Nonetheless, hiding A's code/history does not create statistically
independent semantics, especially with the same model, guide and held records.
https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07962v1

Official code pin 30a2c0aa88af3f428f1f7034538373d24c209176 (29 Mar 2026):
https://github.com/nikhgarg/llm_correlated_errors_public/tree/30a2c0aa88af3f428f1f7034538373d24c209176
The inspected analyze_model_correlations implementation divides identical-wrong
counts by the BOTH-WRONG count. Use that precise definition rather than an
ambiguous README description. The pinned code is later than the paper snapshot;
this review did not rerun the analysis or claim exact reproduction.

5. Positive mechanism evidence, with substantial construction and budget limits

Zhao et al., When Debate Helps, first 3 Oct 2026, inspected v1. It separates correct
proposal supply from readout recovery and reports gains for coverage-selected
societies on two small backbones, including some frozen out-of-domain transfer.
It also measures damage to initially correct votes: more surfaced correct answers
do not guarantee better final selection. Main comparisons are vote/debate-call
aligned, not a demonstrated match to our all-input/output/tool budget. Construction
profiles hundreds of weight-perturbed agents; labeled and agreement-proxy versions
are distinct. Reported seed-variability intervals are not patient/question-sampling
intervals. This is positive but narrow/new evidence, not a reason to import
weight perturbations, construction sweeps or benchmark answer clustering now.
https://arxiv.org/html/2610.04686v1

Author repo pin 899b2834acd13f7b3298eb88233d39ab419d7a15 (8 Oct 2026):

selection.py distinguishes label-based correctness from label-free answer support;

Concrete interpretation once V6 is complete and integrity/scoring are frozen

Separate the primary's ACTUALLY RELEASED A from an unreleased computed draft.
For eligible disagreements, useful correction mass is A-wrong/B-correct/chooseB;
harm mass is A-correct/B-wrong/chooseB. Wrong/wrong switches cannot create typed
credit; correct/correct switches cannot increase it. Report A-ineligible coverage,
B execution/source/packet eligibility, exact agreement, disagreements, UNKNOWN,
switches and auxiliary fatal/provider losses on the full 300 denominator.
Posthoc oracle availability is a diagnosis of proposal supply, never routing.
This decomposition is our bookkeeping implication, not a semantic verifier.

Keep two comparisons separate: A 175k/27 versus the fresh Single 200k/30, and final
team versus that control. A team's auxiliary gain can coexist with a net loss if
primary capacity was sacrificed. Different stochastic trajectories prevent a
causal budget attribution from one comparison alone. V6's shared 200k/30 ceilings
also permit 32 versus 30 model attempts; equal ceilings do not mean equal actual
tokens, input replay, latency or compute. Preserve that disclosed distinction.

If the completed experiment fails, choose the next investigation from mechanisms:
- Little new eligible B correctness: there is little recoverable proposal supply;
  polishing verbal selection or adding voters has no demonstrated opportunity.
- Useful B alternatives but net harmful switches: selection is the bottleneck;
  judge preference accuracy and source validity are insufficient evidence of
  task-correct choice. Establish a general observable signal before another role.
- Mostly loss in actual A before the auxiliary: first investigate preserving
  solving capacity/trajectory rather than attributing failure to agent count.
- Mainly mechanical/provider accounting losses: separate infrastructure from
  semantics; do not use conditional successful-case accuracy to declare a win.

These are post-completion research decisions, not per-question, template or model
runtime routes. Do not modify the live run. Do not tune the existing 300 endlessly
and call that transfer. A development win still needs the prespecified once-only
question-confirmation comparison. Future model/task studies should freeze the
same orchestration, declare adapters/capability requirements and compare each
against its own capable Single under explicit budgets. Model-family names,
English clinical phrasing and this benchmark's clock convention are not generic
success mechanisms. No paper here proves new-patient, other-QA or cross-model
benefit for V6, and no new experiment or implementation is selected by this memo.
