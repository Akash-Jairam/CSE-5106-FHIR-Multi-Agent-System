FHIR-AgentBench provenance and model payload audit

Clinical source: deidentified records from real patients, not a simulated patient
population. PhysioNet identifies MIMIC-IV Clinical Database Demo on FHIR 2.1.0 as
a 100-patient subset converted from MIMIC-IV/ED demo 2.2. Its ethics statement
identifies deidentified Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center data. The benchmark
paper explicitly grounds its questions in real, deidentified MIMIC-IV-FHIR demo
records. Its Synthea discussion describes other benchmarks. Benchmark questions
are clinician-derived EHRSQL constructions with restored anchor years and clinical
terms; transformed identifiers/dates and synthetic unit-test fixtures do not
make the clinical source population synthetic.
Sources: https://physionet.org/content/mimic-iv-fhir-demo/2.1.0/
https://arxiv.org/html/2509.19319v1 (sections 1 and 3; comparison table)
https://github.com/glee4810/FHIR-AgentBench (author repository/readme)

Local evidence: README line 54 and manifests/starting_packets.source.json point
to that exact PhysioNet release. All 24 downloaded NDJSON gzip files match their
recorded SHA-256 values. The local Patient file contains 100 patients, and all
94 patients in the 2,931-question benchmark belong to that set. Hash/count proof:
/private/tmp/fhir-public-provenance-local-proof.json. Root's prior live preflight
found every requested patient and constructed bounded starting packets; it did
not record a complete canonical hash comparison of all live clinical values.
Thus recorded local-source integrity is verified, while identity/count agreement
alone does not prove that every live-store value equals an unchanged public file.
No additional live-store requests were made for this audit.

Outbound model content: curated question, pseudonymous patient identifiers and
assumed current time; an initial pruned FHIR excerpt capped at 8,000 tokens; then
bounded retrieved-resource/tool previews, computed values, citations, execution
diagnostics and source-check feedback. These can include demographics, shifted
event dates, encounter/ward details, diagnoses, measurements, microbiology and
medication/dose data. V3's contract gets question/context; its static auditor gets
contract, executed code, workspace identity, retrieval ledger and bounded source
samples. The rendered solver answer is deliberately omitted from that auditor.
The local Azure adapter forwards messages/tools to AzureOpenAI chat completions.
Bounds and gzip audit storage do not remove patient-derived content from prompts.

Hidden from agents: reference true_answer, SQL queries, gold FHIR-resource lists,
family/template scoring labels and split/perturbation manifest annotations. The
driver's curated prompt uses question, patient_fhir_id and assumption only;
starting-packet selection uses the question and fetched record, not gold answers.
Retrieved source IDs and model-computed answers are visible as ordinary tool data.
Evidence: utils/core_utils.py:265; tools/starting_packet.py; agent/single_agent.py;
agent/query_contract_agent.py; .local-setup/azure_adapter.py:88-103 (code only).

This report describes provenance and transmission, not authorization. Public
availability or the demo's license does not itself replace consent. Root received
the user's explicit model-run consent separately. No secret configurations/API
values were read, no model calls were made, and no source files were changed by
this audit.
