Prospective evaluation amendment: adaptation, confirmation and portability
15 October 2026 UTC; applies to subsequent designs, not V4's historical gate.

Why the evaluation changes

The fixed 300 questions have repeatedly informed architecture selection and
failure analysis. They now serve as development validation. A further win there
is useful for selecting a candidate but cannot independently establish new-QA
generalization. Repeating a run or computing a confidence interval does not undo
the information used to choose the design. This concern applies to prompt and
controller selection even without updating model weights.

The metadata-only exposure audit found 94 patients in the released 2,931
questions. Documented prior development reserves 32 patients; the pilot uses
three more; the adaptively reused 300 cover 58 more. Only one nominally
QA-outcome-unexposed patient remains, with 18 questions, 16 in proposal scope.
That patient was included in an availability preflight. One patient cannot
provide a useful population-level patient-generalization test. Repartitioning
the same release cannot restore a substantial untouched patient holdout.

The audit examined 47 extant historical result files/identities, including
copied archived result files and the V4 development interface check: 321 unique attempted
question IDs across 62 patients. Recorded earlier human/agent development
exposure is broader than those saved run IDs, so all 32 development patients
remain excluded from confirmation. This is an audited workflow history, not
proof that public data were inaccessible or absent from model pretraining.

Metadata-only prospective manifest

manifests/final_evaluation_strata_20261015.json binds exact IDs, source hashes,
and the exposure audit. Its four disjoint strata cover all 2,931 questions:

  Reserved development patients       1,174 questions
  Reserved pilot patients                60 questions
  Adaptively reused questions           300 questions
  Question-disjoint confirmation      1,397 questions

The confirmation stratum includes 380 lookup/count, 639 temporal, 165 numerical
and 213 other questions. Its nested in-scope view has 1,184 questions. Of the
1,397 questions, 1,379 concern 58 already exposed patients and 18 concern the
single nominally outcome-unexposed patient. This tests new question instances
mostly on familiar patient records; it is not patient-disjoint validation.

Templates absent from the known attempted IDs may be described as such. Earlier
development inspected template language, so they are not an untouched-template
test. Patient, question, template, model and dataset generalization are distinct.
The audit and manifest were prepared without reading new question text, answers,
SQL or patient values for sampling. No new subset was run or scored.

Development and selection

Continue substantive architecture comparisons on the existing fixed 300 with
a fresh capable single agent using the same model, shared tools, scorer,
provider policy and aggregate caps. The next candidate's development gate is
strictly more correct answers than both the best completed single (currently
239/300) and its fresh contemporary single. Record every substantial frozen
design and all scheduled first attempts, including negative completed results.
This gate selects a candidate; it is not a confirmatory statistical claim.

Use reserved development/pilot patients for live interface checks and broader
development coverage; all their questions are excluded from confirmation.
Use specification-based synthetic fixtures to vary source records and task
structure. Do not query or inspect confirmation cases during design refinement.
Before final launch, cross-check new run/exposure metadata against the frozen
confirmation IDs. Any newly exposed confirmation cases require explicit
accounting before launch; do not silently keep the original clean-set label.

One final freeze and one confirmation

After development selection, freeze ONE candidate and the contemporary single
control, exact source/runtime/provider identities, all 2,931 input IDs, tools,
shared conventions, model settings, aggregate caps, scorer, exposure manifest
digest, analysis code and decision rules. The original three-arm full benchmark
still runs on all 2,931 questions. Its saved full outputs provide confirmation;
we do not spend another fresh 300-question set on each successive design.

No partial scoring or case inspection of the confirmation stratum is allowed.
Monitor operational metadata only until the full paired arms are terminal and
first-attempt/source/audit integrity checks pass. Keep failures incorrect in
end-to-end accuracy. Never replace a failed question or select the best of
multiple attempts. Operational recovery must preserve the declared attempt
policy and disclose what executed; a provider failure is not evidence of a
semantic design defect or permission to choose a favorable rerun.

The primary confirmatory comparison is team minus contemporary single on the
prespecified 1,397 question IDs. Report the paired accuracy difference and a
95% patient-block bootstrap interval using 10,000 replicates and seed 5106.
The estimand is question-weighted accuracy: resample complete patient clusters,
then divide total paired correct-answer difference by total questions in that
resample. It is not an equal-weight mean of patient accuracies. The scheduled
study has one first attempt per question/arm. Any future declared repetitions
must stay bundled with their question and patient, with no best-attempt choice.
A positive estimate with an interval crossing zero is inconclusive evidence
of improvement. A claim of supported improvement requires a positive lower
bound. Also report the overall 2,931 result; the goal of broad benchmark benefit
requires team accuracy above single on that full release. Keep these results
separate so gains on reused questions cannot hide failure on new questions.

Report all four exposure strata and nested in-scope/other views regardless of
direction. Family/subtype analyses and the single-patient diagnostic are
descriptive; do not select a favorable subgroup to replace the primary result.
Do not calculate a population patient-bootstrap interval from one patient.
Report source/execution support, correction help/harm, terminal failures,
budget refusals, tokens, tools and latency alongside answer accuracy.
The interval is conditional on this cohort and template distribution; patient
blocking does not remove selection bias, establish template independence, or
account for every possible dependency shared across linguistic templates.

If confirmation does not support benefit, record the negative or inconclusive
result. Do not keep refining against the same confirmation outcomes and call
later improvements held-out evidence. Using those outcomes for refinement makes
them development data; another confirmatory claim then requires independently
protected new data and another prospective protocol. Preregistering another
analysis of the spent cases does not restore their original status. Starting or
completing the full run is not by itself evidence that generalization succeeded.

Portability and limits

The current study uses gpt-6-luna only. Keep role protocols, uncertainty/fallback
behavior, tool accounting and correction logic independent of model identity.
Keep FHIR conventions in shared domain adapters. Avoid benchmark-ID routes,
answer caches, reference-program inference or hard-coded repairs for observed
questions. The companion design_generalization_requirements_20261015.txt sets
these implementation constraints.

A later cross-model study must freeze the method before testing additional
model families against their own capable single-agent controls. A cross-task
study needs independent QA data with explicit adapters and comparable controls.
Neither ability is established by the present model's results. FHIRPath-QA uses
the same MIMIC-IV-FHIR demo and is therefore a possible new question/task source,
not an independent patient cohort. A new Synthea cohort could test structural
robustness but would support a synthetic-data claim. A substantial real-patient
external-validity claim requires additional independently sourced patients.

Research basis and interpretation

Cawley and Talbot show that optimizing a finite model-selection criterion can
bias the apparent comparison. Dwork et al. study protected adaptive holdouts;
our openly inspected ordinary scores do not implement their mechanism or inherit
its guarantees. Davis et al. discuss patient overlap in healthcare ML; applying
that concern to human/agent QA adaptation is our methodological inference.
Recht et al. show that fresh-set drops can also reflect distribution differences,
so neither overfitting nor successful transfer should be presumed without data.
