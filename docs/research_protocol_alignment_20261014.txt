Do More Agents Help? Controlled and Protocol-Aligned Evaluation of LLM Agent
Workflows, Fu et al., 4 June 2026:
https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05670v1

The controlled BenchAgent comparison shares loaders, tools, answer contracts,
usage accounting, and trajectory logs. Most tested multi-agent wrappers trail
its single-agent anchor; EvoAgent's small positive average lies within their
single-run uncertainty guidance. Their external GAIA runtime result improves
harder tasks, but Table 11 explicitly says a uniform resource ceiling is not
certified. Its advantage therefore does not establish equal-budget topology
lift. Retained examples suggest lost constraints during linear handoffs and
local recoverability through persistent state; these mechanisms remain bundled.
No author release URL was verified in the paper's HTML despite a release claim.

Local inference: report exact role-visible state, repair gates, contracts, and
caps alongside accuracy. Preserve all question constraints and actual artifacts
across our audit handoff. Our refreshed single control is necessary to distinguish
the shared tool/interface improvement from a team benefit. Patient-block paired
intervals are more appropriate locally than treating related patient questions
as independent observations. The V2 bundle is explicitly exploratory, not a
causal topology ablation; V3 must keep its shared substrate fixed.

Rethinking the Value of Multi-Agent Workflow: A Strong Single Agent Baseline,
Xu et al., 18 January 2026:
https://arxiv.org/html/2601.12307v1

OneFlow investigates compact automatically designed workflows and single-context
execution of homogeneous roles across seven benchmarks. A single implementation
can match its multi-context counterpart while improving efficiency. Its
simulation proposition requires the same role-visible histories, routing,
deterministic tool behavior, and decoding distribution. The implementation appends
role instructions and retains earlier context; it also discusses KV-cache reuse.
Its automatic design search uses additional designer/critic models and benchmark
performance, which differs from our frozen per-question policy and same-model
inference comparison. No author repository was verified from this HTML.

Local inference: roles alone do not justify a team. Our draft-blind verifier and
question-contract auditor deliberately receive different visible histories, so
the identical-history simulation proposition does not establish their behavior
is equivalent to our existing single agent. Conversely, separate calls do not
guarantee useful independence: shared model biases can persist. We should retain
an integrated solver, add only a distinct audit function, and measure net repair
gain, harmful revisions, cost and failures. A future same-context audit ablation
could test the value of context isolation; it is not an outcome already shown.
