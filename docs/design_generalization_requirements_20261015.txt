Generalization requirements for subsequent designs
Recorded prospectively after the completed V4 comparison, 15 October 2026 UTC.

The objective is a reusable multi-agent QA method. A gain on the repeatedly
inspected 300 questions is an architecture-development signal. It cannot by
itself establish generalization to new questions, patients, tasks, or models.
The user's current instruction keeps experiments on gpt-6-luna while requiring
the design to remain portable. Cross-model and cross-task performance are
unmeasured until separately tested; research motivation is not empirical proof.

Architecture constraints

1. The controller must not route by benchmark question ID, patient ID, template
   ID, gold answer, reference SQL, precomputed correctness, or model identity.
   Do not add a special repair rule merely to recover an observed lost point.
   A diagnosed failure may motivate a general hypothesis and development test.

2. Separate reusable orchestration from domain adapters. The reusable method
   handles role isolation, evidence-producing tool use, explicit uncertainty,
   bounded revision, source validation, and accounting. FHIR resource schemas,
   joins, deidentification/time conventions, and clinical terminology belong
   to the shared FHIR adapter. They must not be advertised as universal QA rules.
   Improvements to common tools or conventions also apply to the single agent
   and require a fresh controlled comparison.

3. Use explicit, bounded role inputs and outputs, with host validation and a
   documented fallback for malformed or unavailable auxiliary output. A valid
   primary must not be vetoed by an inconclusive auxiliary model judgment.
   Unknown provider usage and global budget violations remain fatal. Record
   attempted corrections and harmful revisions, not only successful repairs.

4. Every claimed correction needs observable evidence: the question obligation,
   the actual computation or selection that violates it, and the tool/source
   result supporting the replacement. JSON validity, exact textual quotations,
   executable code and correct citations each establish limited properties;
   none independently proves that the question was interpreted correctly.
   Keep those assurance limits explicit rather than calling a critic an oracle.

5. Keep model selection outside the inference policy. Model/deployment IDs are
   configuration. Do not rely on a specific model's private reasoning format,
   hidden confidence, thought tokens, or undocumented response fields. Preserve
   the provider adapter for accounting and transport. Count all role context,
   completions and tools within the common aggregate ceilings. Equal token
   ceilings do not imply equal cost or computation across future models.

6. Use development tests that vary task structure and source contents: event
   order, empty versus missing data, irrelevant records, linked dependencies,
   absolute/relative dates, units, and failed tools. Derive expectations from
   independent synthetic fixtures or specifications, not memorized benchmark
   answers. These tests establish engineering properties, not benchmark accuracy.

Evaluation constraints

7. Preserve every completed substantial design comparison, including V4's
   negative result. Keep infrastructure-only failed attempts local as requested.
   Do not change historical frozen designs or rename reused data as untouched.
   Select architecture on development data only, then freeze one candidate,
   the contemporary single control, scorer, inputs, tools, budgets and analysis.

8. Audit prior exposure before naming any confirmation set. Reserve genuinely
   unused questions for a once-only frozen comparison and disclose patient and
   template overlap. A patient-disjoint population claim requires enough new
   patients; a new random split cannot erase earlier exposure. Never inspect
   partial confirmation accuracy to decide whether to continue or modify code.

9. Report answer accuracy, source/execution support, correction help/harm,
   failures and actual resource use separately. Report paired uncertainty with
   patient clustering for this repeated-patient dataset. Such intervals do not
   remove adaptive selection bias or establish external validity. Predeclare
   exposure and family strata before the final benchmark.

10. After selecting the method on the current model, a later portability study
    should freeze the orchestration and compare team versus a capable single
    agent on additional model families and an independent QA dataset. Keep
    domain adapters and model capability requirements explicit. Rewriting the
    method for every test model or inspecting each test until a win changes the
    research question and consumes those tests as development evidence.

Evidence basis

Cawley and Talbot (JMLR 2010) demonstrate selection bias from optimizing noisy
model-selection criteria; no weight training is needed for architecture choices
to overfit their evaluation criterion:
https://jmlr.org/papers/v11/cawley10a.html

Dwork et al. (NeurIPS 2015) study adaptive holdout reuse and protective mechanisms.
Our transparent ordinary scorer is not a reusable-holdout mechanism and carries
none of its guarantees:
https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.02629

Davis et al. (JAMIA 2024) discuss aggregation-based patient overlap in clinical
ML. The application here is an inference for QA evaluation and human/agent
architecture adaptation, not a claim that these agents were weight-trained:
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10746313/

Recht et al. (ICML 2019) show why a fresh-set performance drop must not be
automatically attributed to adaptive overfitting: distribution differences can
also cause it. We must measure generalization rather than assume either cause:
https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/recht19a.html
